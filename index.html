RAILMESH

Passengers become the network.

No signal doesn't mean no send. RailMesh turns every phone in the coach into a relay, so your file finds its way to its destination — hop by hop — even when the internet has checked out somewhere outside Jhansi.

"When the internet disappears, the network doesn't."

Category Student Innovation Domain Software Team Binary Brigade
The problem

Ever tried sending a photo mid-journey and just… watched the spinner?

Long-distance train routes cut straight through India's connectivity dead zones — stretches where towers thin out and bars disappear for minutes at a time, sometimes longer.

Right now, that means passengers just wait. A ticket screenshot, an ID copy for the TC, a shared playlist for the journey — none of it moves without a working cellular link.

Existing offline-share tools solve the wrong problem: they need two devices in the same room, not a chain of strangers spread across a moving train. RailMesh is built for that chain.

The idea

Turn the whole coach into a mesh

Nobody installs anything extra. Phones find each other, form a temporary network, and start passing files along — no data plan required.

Proposed solution

An offline peer-to-peer mesh network

Passengers' devices automatically discover nearby RailMesh users and form a temporary local network — no router, no setup, no signal needed.

How it beats the problem

Content travels even without internet

A file doesn't need a direct connection between sender and receiver — it just needs a path of nearby phones willing to relay it.

  • No mobile data
  • No internet required

Innovation & uniqueness

Multi-hop offline sharing
  • Beyond direct range — reaches people even far apart
  • Multi-hop delivery — intermediate devices act as relays
  • Self-forming mesh — more passengers, stronger network

Feasibility, challenges & strategies

Every phone, a node

Making offline connectivity practical in real railway networks — not just on a whiteboard.

Technically feasible

Built entirely on smartphone wireless radios and P2P protocols — no cellular or internet dependency.

Operationally feasible

Passengers themselves become relay nodes — no fixed infrastructure, and it works even in dense coaches.

Scalable by design

More peers means more relay paths, which means wider coverage — the network gets stronger as it grows.
